# 🎮 Player Segmentation Analysis

**Unsupervised learning (K-Means) for product and monetization insights in a mobile game.**

Author: **Nelson Nicolás Hernández Becerra**

Tech stack: Python · pandas · scikit-learn · SciPy · Matplotlib · Seaborn

---

## 📖 Overview

This project segments player records of a mobile game using behavioral, social and monetization variables. There is no target variable, so the problem is approached as **unsupervised learning**: the goal is to discover groups of players with different dominant behaviors and translate them into hypotheses for product, growth and monetization decisions.

The analysis is documented step by step in four notebooks, including the decisions taken, the problems found in the data and the limitations of the results.

## 🔎 Key findings

Four segments were identified with K-Means (K = 4) on three standardized features: engagement, social activity and spending.

| Segment | Records | Share of revenue | Distinctive profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High Spenders** | 7.1% | 43.3% | Very high spending per record (~9–11x the other segments) |
| **Engaged Progressors** | 24.0% | 13.8% | Highest level, playtime and login frequency; low social activity |
| **Social Players** | 17.4% | 11.7% | Highest social activity; moderate engagement |
| **Casual Players** | 51.4% | 31.2% | Lowest activity in every indicator, but spending similar to active segments |

Main takeaways:

- A small group of high spenders (7.1% of records) generates 43.3% of the recorded revenue, and 52.8% of them sit exactly at the maximum recorded spending (99.99), which suggests a spending ceiling.
- Being more engaged or more social does **not** translate into a higher payer rate: it stays between 56% and 61% across the three segments that are not high spenders.
- The segments do not differ in device type, Facebook connection or preferred in-game action (Cramér's V < 0.10).
- The solution is **stable** (mean Adjusted Rand Index of 0.988 across 20 random seeds), but the cluster structure is **weak** (silhouette = 0.361). The segments should be read as zones of a continuum of behaviors, not as sharply separated types.

Initial hypotheses: H1, H2 and H4 (pairwise relationships between engagement, social activity, spending and progression) were **not supported**; H3 (existence of distinct segments) was **partially supported**.

## 🗂️ Dataset

The dataset has **2,000 records and 19 variables** covering progression, gameplay activity, monetization, social interaction and device information.

- **Source:** [`datasets/dataset.csv`](https://github.com/pancham8675/player_churn_prediction/blob/main/datasets/dataset.csv) in the public repository [pancham8675/player_churn_prediction](https://github.com/pancham8675/player_churn_prediction).
- **License:** the source repository does not specify a license, so the file is **not redistributed here**. Please download it from the link above and save it as `data/raw/dataset.csv`.

The origin of the data could not be verified. The share of payers (62.2%) is unusually high compared with what is commonly reported for mobile games, which suggests the dataset may be a sample or may have been generated artificially.

## ⚠️ Main limitations

- `Player ID` is not a reliable player-level key (attributes that should be stable change within the same ID), so **each row is treated as an independent record**. Segments describe behavioral profiles of records, not unique individuals.
- Several variables concentrate values at their lower bound (e.g. 45% of records at the minimum `Time Played`), and `Purchase Amount` has a ceiling at 99.99. It cannot be verified whether these reflect real behavior or measurement limits.
- The cluster structure is weak and K-Means is sensitive to extreme values.
- The data is a single snapshot with no time dimension, and the currency of the spending variable is undocumented, so "revenue" means the sum of recorded amounts.
- Recommendations are hypotheses to be validated with controlled experiments, not conclusions.

The complete list is in section 5.9 of notebook 04.
